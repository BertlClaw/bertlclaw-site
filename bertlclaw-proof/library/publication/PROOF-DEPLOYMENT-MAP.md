# Proof Deployment Map

Purpose: decide **where each BertlClaw proof asset type should appear** across website and sales/application surfaces.

This is the missing layer between:
- internal evidence capture
- publication readiness
- actual business deployment on pages, proposals, and follow-up messages

Use this after a project has at least a minimum evidence pack.

---

## 1. Surfaces covered

- **Homepage** — fast trust, low-friction credibility, short attention span
- **Services pages** — service-specific reassurance and concrete deliverables
- **Proof page** — deepest proof layer, evidence logic, mini-cases, reusable modules
- **Proposals / offers** — tailored reassurance matched to the buyer's exact situation
- **Follow-up** — lightweight proof reinforcement after inquiry, call, or proposal send

---

## 2. Core rule

Not every proof asset belongs everywhere.

A strong proof system does **not** mean dumping the whole archive onto every surface.

Use this logic:
- **Homepage** = shortest, broadest, highest-signal proof
- **Services** = proof tied to the specific offer
- **Proof page** = fuller evidence and mini-cases
- **Proposals** = selected proof matched to the prospect's bottleneck
- **Follow-up** = 1-2 highly relevant assets only

Compact relevance beats proof clutter.

---

## 3. Asset-type deployment matrix

## A. Hero before/after

### Best use
- Homepage
- Proof page
- Proposals
- Follow-up

### Why
This is one of the fastest ways to make improvement visible in seconds.

### Placement guidance
- **Homepage:** teaser or compact comparison reference, never a giant wall
- **Services:** only on pages where copy/positioning/landingpage clarity is relevant
- **Proof page:** full explanation with context
- **Proposals:** use when the prospect's issue is unclear messaging or weak first impression
- **Follow-up:** send as a single image or short text comparison after a call

### Avoid when
- the difference is subtle
- the old version is confidential
- the improvement needs too much explanation to land quickly

---

## B. Offer-structure cleanup

### Best use
- Services pages
- Proof page
- Proposals

### Why
This proves BertlClaw can turn scattered material into readable logic.

### Placement guidance
- **Homepage:** mention only indirectly as a short proof bullet
- **Services:** excellent fit near deliverables or process section
- **Proof page:** show structure before/after or section logic example
- **Proposals:** strong when the buyer has too many offers, sections, or mixed messages
- **Follow-up:** use as a short outline or annotated screenshot if structure confusion was the core issue

### Avoid when
- there is no clean visual or readable summary
- the project type was not structure-heavy

---

## C. FAQ / objection block

### Best use
- Services pages
- Proposals
- Follow-up
- Deliverable gallery on proof page

### Why
It shows concrete trust-building work rather than vague strategy language.

### Placement guidance
- **Homepage:** only as a brief proof line, not a full block
- **Services:** very strong near CTA or objection-handling section
- **Proof page:** include as a deliverable example or mini-case artifact
- **Proposals:** ideal when the prospect is hesitant or needs reassurance
- **Follow-up:** useful as a pasted snippet when someone asked a repeatable question

### Avoid when
- the text is too context-specific to another client
- the excerpt reveals confidential information

---

## D. CTA logic upgrade

### Best use
- Homepage
- Services pages
- Proof page
- Proposals

### Why
CTA improvements are easy to understand and tightly linked to conversion clarity without needing risky numeric claims.

### Placement guidance
- **Homepage:** short proof badge or mini-example
- **Services:** strong near "what gets improved" or deliverables section
- **Proof page:** explain the friction that was removed
- **Proposals:** useful when the prospect's site has weak next-step framing
- **Follow-up:** send if the call revealed friction around next steps, inquiry flow, or mobile contact

---

## E. Messaging map / copy system

### Best use
- Services pages
- Proof page
- Proposals

### Why
This shows strategic thinking translated into usable output.

### Placement guidance
- **Homepage:** mention only at a high level
- **Services:** very strong for positioning/text offers
- **Proof page:** can carry more context and show excerpts
- **Proposals:** excellent when the buyer needs language clarity, not just page polish
- **Follow-up:** share only if the prospect clearly values strategic structure

### Avoid when
- the artifact is too abstract for a cold prospect
- the sales stage needs faster, more obvious proof

---

## F. Priority / documentation / system artifact

### Best use
- Services pages
- Proof page
- Proposals
- Follow-up

### Why
This is central for the "digitale Ordnung & Klarheit" offer and makes invisible operations work more tangible.

### Placement guidance
- **Homepage:** use as one compact line or card in a proof/offer area
- **Services:** strong on structure/system-related services
- **Proof page:** show as a visible system transformation example
- **Proposals:** especially useful when prospects are overwhelmed by files, tasks, or unclear priorities
- **Follow-up:** send as a simple board / map / system snapshot if it matches the conversation exactly

---

## G. Final implementation snapshot

### Best use
- Homepage
- Services pages
- Proof page
- Proposals
- Follow-up

### Why
Final shipped output connects thinking to a real result.

### Placement guidance
- **Homepage:** use selectively as polished trust evidence
- **Services:** strong as "what this can look like"
- **Proof page:** pair with explanation of what changed
- **Proposals:** strong if the prospect wants confidence that BertlClaw actually ships
- **Follow-up:** excellent after sending an offer, because it reassures without over-explaining

### Avoid when
- the output looks nice but does not demonstrate the specific problem solved

---

## H. Mini-case

### Best use
- Proof page
- Proposals
- Follow-up
- sometimes services pages

### Why
Mini-cases carry the full logic chain: before, intervention, deliverables, visible difference, evidence.

### Placement guidance
- **Homepage:** teaser only
- **Services:** short service-relevant excerpt only
- **Proof page:** primary home
- **Proposals:** one or two mini-cases matched to the prospect's situation
- **Follow-up:** send the shortest relevant case, not a whole library

---

## I. Deliverable-gallery card

### Best use
- Homepage
- Services pages
- Proof page
- Proposals

### Why
These cards show what clients actually get in hand.

### Placement guidance
- **Homepage:** 3-6 cards max, focused on breadth and clarity
- **Services:** pick only cards native to that service
- **Proof page:** fuller gallery or linked proof types
- **Proposals:** use as a compact "what you will likely receive" block
- **Follow-up:** usually not needed unless a prospect asked what the output looks like

---

## J. Client-approved quote or comment

### Best use
- Homepage
- Services pages
- Proof page
- Proposals
- Follow-up

### Why
Human voice can add trust fast, but only as a support layer.

### Placement guidance
- Keep it short, specific, and restrained
- Pair with a visible artifact whenever possible
- Never let testimonial language replace evidence

### Avoid when
- approval is vague
- the quote is generic fluff

---

## 4. Surface-by-surface deployment rules

## Homepage

### Goal
Build trust quickly without slowing the page down.

### Best asset types
- final implementation snapshot
- hero before/after teaser
- deliverable-gallery cards
- 1-3 proof badges or honest proof statements
- short client-approved quote if strong

### Should usually appear here
- short honest proof statement
- 3 compact deliverable or proof cards
- link to the deeper proof page
- proof language that feels concrete, not inflated

### Should usually not live here
- long case studies
- too many screenshots
- dense internal process explanations
- full messaging maps or long structure diagrams

---

## Services pages

### Goal
Reduce service-specific buying friction.

### Best asset types
- deliverable-gallery cards related to the service
- offer-structure examples
- FAQ / objection block example
- messaging map excerpt
- system artifact if relevant
- final implementation snapshot

### Rule
Every service page should answer:
1. what gets delivered
2. what visible difference this creates
3. what proof asset supports that claim

---

## Proof page

### Goal
Be the deepest public trust layer.

### Best asset types
- mini-cases
- proof logic explanation
- proof atoms
- deliverable gallery
- before/after pairs
- structure comparisons
- publication rules / trust logic

### Rule
The proof page should hold the **fuller evidence story** so homepage and service pages can stay lean.

---

## Proposals / offers

### Goal
Turn generalized trust into buyer-specific confidence.

### Best asset types
- 1-2 mini-cases similar to the prospect's situation
- 2-4 deliverable-gallery cards matching the proposed work
- one before/after or final implementation image
- one short proof bullet list
- optional quote if tightly relevant

### Rule
Do not attach a generic proof dump.

Instead select proof using this filter:
- same bottleneck
- same project type
- same deliverable shape
- same desired next step

---

## Follow-up

### Goal
Keep momentum after inquiry, call, or proposal without overwhelming the prospect.

### Best asset types
- one mini-case
- one screenshot or before/after
- one FAQ or objection snippet
- one deliverable preview

### Rule
A follow-up should normally include **only 1-2 assets** plus a short explanation of relevance.

Example logic:
- after a discovery call -> send the closest mini-case
- after proposal send -> send one proof image + one short reminder of deliverables
- after hesitation -> send FAQ/objection block or proof bullet list

## 6. Selection rule by sales stage

## Early interest
Use:
- homepage proof
- service-page proof
- short deliverable gallery

## Qualified conversation
Use:
- one mini-case
- one artifact matched to the bottleneck

## Proposal sent
Use:
- 1-2 matched proof assets
- proof bullet list reinforcing deliverables and trust

## Stalled / hesitant
Use:
- FAQ snippet
- approval-safe screenshot
- one short case showing similar uncertainty resolved into clarity

---

## 7. Minimum deployment bundle per strong project

A strong approved project should ideally feed:
- **Homepage:** 1 teaser-worthy proof line or card
- **Services:** 1 service-specific deliverable or proof example
- **Proof page:** 1 full mini-case or artifact set
- **Proposal use:** 1 tailored snippet or image
- **Follow-up use:** 1 lightweight sendable asset

If a project only lives in the archive, the proof system is underperforming.

---

## 8. Practical deployment checklist

Before deploying a proof asset to any surface, confirm:
- [ ] approval status is documented
- [ ] asset matches the target surface's attention span
- [ ] claim is visible, approved, or measurable
- [ ] asset is relevant to the exact service or sales context
- [ ] confidential details are removed
- [ ] mobile readability is acceptable if used on website or phone follow-up

---

## 9. Recommendation

Think of proof assets like a publishing stack:

- **Homepage** earns initial trust
- **Services** answer "can they do this specific thing?"
- **Proof page** answers "is this credible and real?"
- **Proposal** answers "why this is the right fit for me"
- **Follow-up** keeps the deal moving with compact relevant evidence

That is how proof becomes a working business tool instead of a dead archive.

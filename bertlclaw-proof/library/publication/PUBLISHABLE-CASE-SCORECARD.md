# Publishable Case Scorecard

Use this to decide whether an internal proof example or real project is ready to become a **public anonymized case**.

Goal: avoid publishing material that is vague, overclaimed, weakly evidenced, or too revealing.

---

## Quick decision rule

A case is **publishable anonymized** only if all 4 gates pass:

1. **Permission gate** — anonymous public use is explicitly allowed
2. **Evidence gate** — the case has visible proof, not only narrative
3. **Anonymization gate** — no brand/client-identifying detail leaks unintentionally
4. **Claim gate** — every public sentence is visible, approved, or measurably true

If one gate fails, the case stays internal.

---

## Scoring model

Score each category from 0 to 3.

- **0 = missing / unsafe**
- **1 = weak / partial**
- **2 = solid**
- **3 = strong / publishable-quality**

### 1. Approval clarity
- **0** no approval status recorded
- **1** approval is implied but not written
- **2** anonymous-public approval is written
- **3** anonymous-public approval is written with any boundaries clearly noted

### 2. Starting situation clarity
- **0** context is vague
- **1** broad problem described, but fuzzy
- **2** starting situation is understandable in 2-4 concrete bullets
- **3** starting situation is precise, relatable, and easy to visualize

### 3. Bottleneck sharpness
- **0** no clear core problem
- **1** too many problems mixed together
- **2** main bottleneck is clearly named
- **3** bottleneck is sharp enough that the intervention feels obvious and credible

### 4. Work specificity
- **0** only generic phrases like "optimized" or "improved"
- **1** some actions listed, but still fluffy
- **2** concrete BertlClaw actions are named
- **3** actions are specific enough to sound premium and believable

### 5. Deliverable concreteness
- **0** no visible outputs named
- **1** outputs mentioned in broad categories only
- **2** deliverables are clearly listed
- **3** deliverables feel tangible, differentiated, and reusable as proof atoms

### 6. Visible difference
- **0** no before/after difference stated
- **1** only generic improvement language
- **2** visible difference is clearly stated
- **3** visible difference is easy to understand without extra explanation

### 7. Evidence quality
- **0** no usable evidence
- **1** evidence exists but is weak or indirect
- **2** at least one good proof asset exists
- **3** strong proof set exists: screenshot, text diff, structure comparison, approved excerpt, or final artifact

### 8. Anonymization safety
- **0** identifiable details remain or are not checked
- **1** obvious identifiers removed, but uniqueness risk remains
- **2** names, logos, URLs, contacts, and unique markers removed
- **3** case is safely anonymized and still understandable after masking

### 9. Claim discipline
- **0** overclaims, implied metrics, or unsupported business results present
- **1** wording still leans too hard
- **2** wording is restrained and evidence-backed
- **3** wording is precise, premium, and legally/commercially safe

### 10. Reuse value
- **0** only useful once
- **1** limited reuse potential
- **2** useful for website or proposals
- **3** can feed multiple surfaces: proof page, homepage, proposals, outreach, sales snippets, deliverable gallery

---

## Decision thresholds

### 24-30 points — publishable now
Safe to turn into a public anonymized mini-case once final draft and assets are assembled.

### 18-23 points — near-publishable
Worth finishing. Usually missing sharper wording, better evidence selection, or stronger anonymization.

### 12-17 points — internal only for now
Keep as an internal example or proof seed. Do not publish yet.

### 0-11 points — not useful enough yet
Needs major reconstruction or should remain only a process example.

---

## Mandatory fail conditions

Even with a high score, do **not** publish if any of these are true:

- no explicit anonymous-public approval
- screenshots still reveal names, domains, email addresses, phone numbers, location markers, or unique branding
- text implies revenue / leads / conversion lifts without measurement
- case depends on confidential strategy or sensitive internal data
- the public angle would feel misleading without details that cannot be shared

---

## Minimum publication bar

For a compact BertlClaw anonymized case, require at least:

- Approval clarity: **2+**
- Work specificity: **2+**
- Deliverable concreteness: **2+**
- Visible difference: **2+**
- Evidence quality: **2+**
- Anonymization safety: **2+**
- Claim discipline: **2+**
- Total score: **18+**

Anything below that stays internal.

## Suggested reviewer mindset

Ask:

- Would a skeptical prospect still find this believable?
- Is the work specific enough to feel real?
- Is the proof visible enough to earn trust?
- Does anonymity hold even for someone familiar with the client's niche?
- Does the case make BertlClaw look sharper without sounding inflated?

If yes, it passes the spirit of the scorecard, not just the math.
